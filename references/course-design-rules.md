# Course Design Rules

## Audience and purpose

This workflow assumes Chinese university students whose general English proficiency may be lower than the level of the professional source text. The deck therefore serves three functions at once:

1. preserve accurate disciplinary English;
2. provide enough Chinese scaffolding for comprehension;
3. support the teacher's classroom pacing, questions, media, and explanation.

## Textbook alignment

- Match the assigned unit sequence and lesson objectives.
- Use textbook terminology as the default vocabulary.
- When simplifying an English definition, keep the original expression nearby or in notes.
- Label off-textbook content as “拓展 / Extension”.
- Do not turn a textbook lesson into a general-interest talk.

## Bilingual patterns

Choose the shortest structure that preserves meaning:

- term — Chinese equivalent — one-line explanation;
- English sentence followed by a Chinese logic map;
- bilingual comparison table;
- English process labels with a Chinese mechanism summary;
- prompt in English with a Chinese task instruction.

Avoid two full paragraphs that say the same thing. A normal content page should expose both languages without doubling the reading load.

## Projection typography

- Main title: 32–42 px.
- Main body: normally 24–28 px.
- Important terms or short prompts: 28–34 px when space permits.
- Secondary labels or sources on slide: no smaller than 16 px.
- Use one consistent body-size band across the lesson; allow small variation for density.
- Use generous but controlled line spacing, normally 1.20–1.40.
- Prefer left alignment for instructional text.
- Remove first-line indentation if it produces an irregular left edge.

The projection target is more important than laptop-screen compactness.

## Page fullness and whitespace

The teacher's preferred style is informative and relatively full, not sparse. Still, a slide should not become a document page.

- If a page feels empty, first increase body size and line spacing.
- Then enlarge or reposition the main text frame.
- Add one meaningful photo, process diagram, or example only when it supports the concept.
- If a page is crowded, remove repetition or split it before shrinking the text.
- Keep the top content close enough to the title to feel connected.

## Color and visual consistency

- Default title and navigation color: blue, preferably the deck's existing blue.
- Body: black or dark gray.
- Red: warning, error, negative example, critical contrast, or very limited emphasis.
- Avoid mixed font weights unless the difference encodes hierarchy.
- Use authentic civil-engineering and smart-construction visuals when possible.
- Crop deliberately, keep image aspect ratio, and never cover instructional text.
- Spread visuals through the lesson instead of clustering them in one section.

## Video-page contract

For each candidate video, use a separate page containing:

1. Chinese and English title.
2. Platform and verified URL.
3. Total duration or suggested excerpt.
4. Three preview terms.
5. “Before watching / 观看前” question.
6. “While watching / 观看中” observation task.
7. “After watching / 观看后” output task.

For a 90-minute class, prepare 8–10 candidates so the teacher can select in advance. The actual played set may be smaller.

## Speaker-note contract

Every slide should contain:

- `【教师说】` natural spoken explanation;
- `【课堂操作】` question, pause, pair work, read-aloud, or transition;
- `【时间建议】` approximate duration;
- `【板书/强调】` optional keywords or structure;
- `[Sources]` with one source per line.

Notes should explain the teaching logic of the final summary page in particular; do not end with slogans that are unclear to the teacher.
