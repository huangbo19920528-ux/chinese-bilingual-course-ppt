# Research Basis and Deliberate Adaptation

This skill combines public presentation workflows with the teacher's repeated classroom feedback. External defaults are adapted to Chinese bilingual professional teaching rather than copied mechanically.

## Public workflows reviewed

- Anthropic presentation skill: <https://github.com/anthropics/skills/blob/main/skills/pptx/SKILL.md>
  - Adopted: inspect the entire deck, render after edits, use montages, and verify the saved package.
- `presentation-skill`: <https://github.com/siril9/presentation-skill>
  - Adopted: keep a reusable source-first workspace, explicit layout constraints, and repeatable QA.
- `academic-pptx-skill`: <https://github.com/Gabberflast/academic-pptx-skill>
  - Adopted: each slide has a clear job and slide titles should form a coherent argument.
  - Not adopted as a default: extreme minimalism and one-exhibit-per-slide, because classroom teaching sometimes needs denser bilingual scaffolding.
- `codex-ppt-skill`: <https://github.com/ningzimu/codex-ppt-skill>
  - Adopted: speaker-script preparation and slide-by-slide visual review.
  - Not adopted: image-only PPT output, because the teacher needs editable text and links.
- `nature-skills`: <https://github.com/Yuan1z0825/nature-skills>
  - Adopted: evidence and figure provenance, especially for technical material.

## Instructional evidence reviewed

- Oxford guidance for accessible lecture slides: <https://www.ctl.ox.ac.uk/node/1422351>
  - Supports larger headings and body text for lecture-room viewing.
- University of Waterloo teaching guidance: <https://uwaterloo.ca/centre-for-teaching-excellence/catalogs/tip-sheets/powerpoint>
  - Supports scaling type for the classroom and projection context.
- Multimedia learning segmenting and pre-training principle: <https://doi.org/10.1017/9781108894333.025>
  - Supports teaching key terms before complex processes and splitting long media into usable segments.
- Meta-analysis of subtitles in second-language learning: <https://doi.org/10.3390/educsci13030274>
  - Supports purposeful subtitle use while recognizing that benefit depends on proficiency and task design.

## Teacher-specific evidence retained

Repeated course reviews established these stable preferences:

- textbook consistency is more important than external novelty;
- most instructional pages should be bilingual;
- large paragraph screenshots should become editable text;
- pages should feel reasonably full, with larger type and line spacing;
- titles and navigation should be blue, with red reserved for contrast;
- images should enrich empty space but never cover text;
- videos should appear on separate pages, with about ten candidates per lesson;
- every slide should have classroom-ready notes;
- final slides need plain-language explanation in notes;
- all lessons must be checked consistently, not only the page that exposed the problem.
